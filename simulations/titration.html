Perform acid-base titrations. Track pH in real time and identify the equivalence point.

How Titration Works

A solution of known concentration (the titrant) is added to a solution of unknown concentration (the analyte) until the reaction is complete. The equivalence point is reached when moles of acid = moles of base.

Equivalence Point

MaVa = MbVb

For a 1:1 reaction, moles of acid = moles of base at the equivalence point. The pH at this point depends on the acid/base strength.

pH at Equivalence

Strong-Strong: pH = 7 (neutral)
Weak Acid + Strong Base: pH > 7 (basic)
Strong Acid + Weak Base: pH < 7 (acidic)
The indicator should change colour near the equivalence point pH.

Choosing an Indicator

An indicator is only useful if its transition range sits inside the steep jump at the equivalence point. Switch indicators and watch the shaded band on the curve:

Methyl Orange (3.1–4.4) — acid-side titrations
Bromothymol Blue (6.0–7.6) — strong/strong
Phenolphthalein (8.2–10.0, colourless → pink) — base-side titrations

No pH Meter

There is deliberately no pH readout, curve, or scale anywhere in this simulation. Just like the bench, your only evidence is the colour of the indicator in the flask, and your titre is whatever the burette read at the moment the colour changed.

Titrant can only be added, never withdrawn — an overshoot is a real mistake, and you have to start over with New Sample.

Try This

Run a Weak + Weak titration. The colour barely moves at the endpoint, so the reading you take is genuinely a judgement call — which is why that pairing is rarely used in practice. Then try Weak + Strong, where one drop produces a dramatic change.
